Where this comes from

The guide never tells you the right play. It tells you what to ask.

Every chapter ends with a question. Its last line says the whole point: stop asking “what is the correct play?” and start asking “why is this the correct play?” Pick a chapter.

The gap

The Dojo shows you the board. It never checks your read of it.

It is already a great sandbox: branching timelines, Duels.ink replays, draw odds, a challenge forecast. But practice without feedback is repetition. You can replay a turn a hundred times and never learn that your mistake was the order you played in.

The idea

You answer first. Then the board answers back.

The same learning loop every coach uses: commit to a read, act, get told the truth, repeat. The Dojo already has the board, so it can tell the truth about it.

1 · ReadAskWho wins if nothing changes? What matters on their side?
2 · ActPlayMove cards in the sandbox as you do today. Nothing is blocked.
3 · CheckSeeThe clock, the threats and your exposure, computed from the real board.
4 · ReviewLearnAfter the turn: the order you played in. After the game: where it turned.

Every number is a count, a turn clock or a draw-odds calculation you can check by hand. No win percentages, no “best move” engine. The Dojo stays a sandbox.

Tool 1 · Race Clock · every turn

See the race, not just the score.

“Am I winning this race, or do I need to slow it down?”Quest or Challenge · Playing from ahead or behind

A strip in the HUD that counts who reaches 20 first if every character quests every turn. It updates after every move, so you see what a quest or a challenge actually does to the race.

For the player: your role (race or defend) stops being a feeling. Compare three lines and watch the clock move.

Tool 2 · Threat & Exposure Map · every turn

Know what matters, and what’s dead.

“What happens if I don’t answer this?”Threat Assessment · Virtual card advantage

Their cards get a two-turn cost and a list of what they unlock. Yours get the odds of being removed next turn, counted from the cards you haven’t seen. Ward, Evasive, Support, songs and the Device are all respected.

For the player: you stop removing the biggest card and start removing the one that wins them the game. The “biggest threat isn’t always on the board” lesson, made visible.

Tool 3 · Mulligan Lab · before the game

Grade your mulligan against every other one.

“Which hand gives me the highest chance of executing my game plan?”The Mulligan · Resource management

All 128 ways to mulligan the hand, each played through turn 4 hundreds of times from the same shuffles. Ranked by ink spent on curve, with your plan card protected from the inkwell.

For the player: objective feedback on the decision you make every single game. This Phillip hand has two bombs and no 2-drop. Tap cards to send them back.

Tool 4 · Sequencing Coach · after the turn

Same plays. Better order.

“The strongest players don’t necessarily make better plays. Very often, they simply make the same plays in a better order.”Sequencing

This is turn 13 as recorded: the right five actions in the wrong order. Move the steps. The coach replays the turn each time and flags what you gave away.

For the player: the one mistake you can’t see on your own. The sandbox knows the deck, so it tells you what you would have drawn before inking.

Why it’s sound

Small, checkable, and it never plays for you.

96.6%

of the cards whose text removes, damages, bounces or exerts a character are parsed by one small classifier. That’s 255 of 264 in the full database, and most misses only hit your own side.

0

win percentages. Every number is a count, a turn clock or a hypergeometric you can do on paper, with its assumption stated.

0

rules enforced. The lenses read the board and never block a move. The Dojo stays a sandbox and you stay the referee.

Hidden information is handled the way the Draw Odds pane already does it: their hand is treated as random from the cards you haven’t seen. No new storage either. Everything rides on bookmarks, turn nodes and the action log.
